# Subway IRL — Audio

**Date:** 2026-09-18
**Status:** Chosen by the user as the next phase.
**Extends:** `2026-09-16-subway-irl-design.md` §6.6.

## Why

The game is silent. For a game you play with your body, sound is not decoration: it gives you a
tempo to jog to, tells you the TT is closing without making you read the screen, and rewards a
coin line you never looked at. It is the biggest remaining gap between this and feeling finished.

## Ground rules

- **Real samples first, synthesis as the fallback.** (User: "Get audio samples from online as much
  as you can instead of recreating… otherwise go ahead and create it.") A recorded whistle, coin,
  crowd or tabla sounds better than anything we can synthesise, so search for a free one first and
  only synthesise what cannot be found at an acceptable licence and quality.
- **Licence discipline is absolute.** Only **CC0 / public-domain** sources by default (Freesound
  CC0-filtered, OpenGameArt CC0, Pixabay, Mixkit and similar). CC-BY is acceptable only when the
  attribution is recorded. Anything with a non-commercial, no-derivatives, "credit the artist by
  name in-app" or unclear licence is rejected — as are rips from games, films or music.
  Every file is logged in `public/audio/CREDITS.md` with source URL, author and licence, exactly
  like `public/models/CREDITS.md`.
- **Files live in `public/audio/`** and are committed (they are small and freely licensed, unlike
  the user's local-only rips in `assets/`, which must never be used here). Budget: **≤ 4 MB total**,
  mono where it does not hurt, trimmed, loudness-matched and converted with `ffmpeg` (both `.ogg`
  and `.m4a`/`.mp3` so every browser is covered).
- **Synthesis stays as the safety net.** Every cue must still make a sound when its file is missing
  or fails to decode: the Web Audio synth path is the fallback, so a failed fetch degrades the sound
  instead of breaking the game. Tests never need the network or a real device.
- **Indian flavour, honestly done:** tabla/dholak-ish percussion, a tanpura-like drone, a simple
  riff on a pentatonic/raga-flavoured scale — sampled where a good free recording exists,
  synthesised otherwise. Evocative, not a parody, and never a lift from a real recording.
- **Autoplay:** nothing sounds until the first real user gesture (a key, a click, or the start
  gesture). The browser requires it, and a game that blares on load is obnoxious anyway.
- **Never blocks a frame.** No allocation in the per-frame path, voices pooled, and the graph built
  once. Audio must not cost measurable frame time on the Arc 140T.
- **Always escapable:** a mute toggle reachable at all times (**M** is taken by the monitor — use
  another key, and a speaker button in the HUD), plus music/SFX volumes that persist.

## Layers

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Ambience** | Mumbai station bed: distant crowd murmur, a tannoy blip, crows, traffic hum. Continuous, low, filtered by context (louder near a platform chunk). |
| **Music** | A looping bed that follows the run: a drone + percussion groove that adds layers as the game speeds up, drops to almost nothing when paused, and swells for the Josh meter (2× coins). |
| **Jog pulse** | A light percussive tick on the beat, at the tempo the player should be jogging. This is the fitness feature: something to lock your feet onto. It matches the player's own cadence when they are jogging steadily, and gently leads them back when they drift slow. |
| **SFX** | Coin pickup (rising pitch on a streak), power-up grabs, hoverboard whoosh, jump/land, roll scrape, stumble, train pass-by doppler, TT whistle, "pakda gaya" sting, countdown beeps, UI clicks, calibration/move-check confirmations. |
| **Chaser cue** | The TT's whistle and footsteps, mixed by how close he is — the player should feel him gaining without looking. |

## Architecture

```
src/audio/types.ts     Cue names, the sound-event contract, mixer settings   (contract, written first)
src/audio/manifest.ts  Every cue → its sample file(s) + its synth fallback patch
src/audio/loader.ts    Fetch + decodeAudioData, cached, lazy, falls back to synth on any failure
src/audio/engine.ts    Web Audio graph: master → buses (music/sfx/ambience), limiter, mute
src/audio/voices.ts    The synth patches: percussion, drone, riff, noise/whoosh, whistle, chimes
src/audio/director.ts  PURE: game/pose state + events → cues to fire. No Web Audio, fully testable
src/audio/index.ts     Wiring: a small facade the app calls once per frame
public/audio/          The downloaded, trimmed, loudness-matched files + CREDITS.md
audio-lab.html         A harness page to audition every cue and the music layers, with no game
```

**Loading policy:** the UI cues and the first run's essentials are fetched on the first user
gesture; the rest stream in behind them. A cue that has not loaded yet plays its synth fallback
rather than nothing, so the first seconds of a run are never silent.

The split matters: `director.ts` holds every decision (what plays, when, how loud, what ducks what)
and is unit-tested with a fake sink; `engine.ts`/`voices.ts` hold the sound making. Tests never need
a real audio device, and the lab page lets a human listen.

## Behaviour

- **Ducking:** SFX duck the music slightly; the caught sting ducks everything.
- **Countdown:** three beeps and a higher "GO".
- **Idle warning:** when the idle clock passes the warning point, the music thins and a heartbeat
  comes up — the audio equivalent of the red vignette, so you feel the 2 s deadline.
- **"About to be caught" alarm** (2026-09-18, the user: "when person is close to getting caught play
  this sequence of sounds once — security whistle > 3 bark sounds"): the TT's whistle, then his dog
  barking three times 0.3 s apart (`ALARM` in `director.ts`), each bark pitched slightly differently
  so they do not sound like one sample looped. It plays once per danger — on the idle warning and on
  a stumble, the two moments the chaser closes in — is never restarted while it is still playing,
  and holds off the TT's own periodic whistle until it is done. It replaces the old one-shot warning
  sting. Resting on a hoverboard is allowed, so it never sounds then. The bark is a CC0 recording
  (see `public/audio/CREDITS.md`) with a synthesised fallback.
- **Caught:** everything cuts to the sting, then silence, then the summary bed.
- **Pause / tab hidden:** suspend the graph, so a backgrounded tab is silent.
- **Move Check:** a soft confirmation per completed move, and a small fanfare on the summary.
- **Intro:** the poster sequence gets its own cues (brush strokes, paper unfurl, whistle) — those
  land when the intro does; leave hooks.

## Settings and persistence

Master / music / SFX volumes and mute persist in `localStorage` (`subway-irl.audio.v1`), loaded at
startup, tolerant of corrupt data. They belong in the settings screen when that exists; for now the
HUD toggle plus URL flags (`?mute`, `?vol=0.5`) are enough.

## Testing

- Unit: the director's decisions (cue selection, ducking, chaser mix by distance, jog-pulse tempo
  tracking, idle-warning escalation, state transitions) against a fake sink; settings persistence
  incl. corrupt data.
- The engine is exercised through an `OfflineAudioContext` where possible: the graph builds, a cue
  renders non-silent audio, mute renders silence, and nothing throws when the context is suspended.
- No test may need a real audio device or a user gesture.
- Manual: the lab page, auditioned by a human.

## Out of scope for now

Voice lines, per-character themes, and a full settings screen (that phase comes next).
